# Core Pillars

These are the setting's **invariants**. Every location, character, curse, relic and concept-art piece must support at least one pillar and contradict none. When two ideas compete, the one that serves the pillars more strongly wins.

---

## Pillar 1: Archaic Industrialism vs. Feral Folklore

**Statement:** Industry here is crude, wet and early: mud, cold iron, black powder, timber siege cranes and open smelting pits. It tears into a primeval forest that fights back with blood curses, thorns and mutation.

**This, not that:**
* Hand-cranked drills, bellows, slag heaps and charcoal smoke — **not** brass gears, airships or polished Victorian steampunk.
* A forest that bleeds sap like blood and grows back over trenches overnight — **not** a passive backdrop that waits to be cut.
* Both sides are physically damaged by contact. Iron burns the old things; the old things rust and rot the iron.

**Test:** Does it look like it was built by hungry people in a hurry, or grown by something old and angry? If it looks clean, designed or decorative, it fails.

---

## Pillar 2: Magic as Body Horror

**Statement:** Fairy-tale magic is biological, contagious and transactional. It lives in flesh, spreads by contact and always takes payment from the body or the mind.

**This, not that:**
* Witches breed mold and bone-rot; tailors stitch living skin; petrification slowly turns flesh to brittle stone — **not** spells, glowing runes or mana.
* Every curse has a **cause**, a **progression** and **visible signs** on the body.
* Every relic has a **price**, and the price is paid by whoever uses it, never by an abstract resource.

**Test:** Can you describe what it does to a body, step by step? If the magic has no physical cost or symptom, it fails.

---

## Pillar 3: Systemic Moral Greyness

**Statement:** Nobody in the valley is good. The Smelter Syndicate, the Court of Rot and the Mirror-Cutters each have coherent, understandable reasons for doing terrible things. Surviving means using all three.

**This, not that:**
* Each faction is right about something and monstrous about something else — **not** a villain faction and a hero faction.
* Folklore creatures are victims of the iron as well as predators — **not** pure evil.
* Help always comes with a hook: a debt, a betrayal, a cost to someone else.

**Test:** Can a sympathetic member of each faction explain, in their own voice, why the other two must be stopped? If one side has no defensible logic, it fails.

---

## Tonal Invariants

* **Grim, not nihilistic.** Hope exists but is rare, expensive and fragile. Small kindnesses matter because they cost something.
* **Folklore played straight.** No winking, no modern irony, no parody of fairy tales.
* **The familiar made wrong.** Known motifs (the gingerbread house, the glass slipper, the mirror) are recognizable at first glance and horrifying on second.
* **Show the cost, not the gore.** Horror comes from process, consequence and implication more than splatter.
* **Germanic grounding.** Names, materials and atmosphere draw on German mining, smithing and folk tradition.

## Anti-Pillars (what this setting is not)

* Not steampunk or gaslamp fantasy.
* Not high fantasy: no elves-and-dwarves races, no chosen-one destiny.
* Not a Mirrorworld fan work (see [DDR-001](../decisions/001-ip-nomenclature.md)).
* Not comedic or self-aware.
